# downtown-vegas-nevada: Downtown Las Vegas and Nevada venues (7)

## Ask
For each venue: confirm or correct opening and closing years; build the name and owner lineage; find sources on organised-crime ties in both directions; say if a label is unsupported.

## Cited findings
- El Cortez opened 7 Nov 1941; "By 1945, mobster Bugsy Siegel had taken over the property" and sold it in 1946 — [Online Nevada](https://www.onlinenevada.org/articles/el-cortez-hotel-casino), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Cortez_(Las_Vegas))
- Golden Nugget opened 1946 under Guy McAfee; later Wynn (1973), MGM (2000), Poster (2003), Landry's (2005) — [Online Nevada](https://www.onlinenevada.org/articles/guy-mcafee), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Nugget_Las_Vegas)
- Fremont opened 1956; Argent owned it from 1974; Nevada Gaming Commission revoked its licence in Aug 1979; Boyd from 1983 — [Sun 1979](https://lasvegassun.com/news/1979/aug/24/state-fines-argent-corp-revokes-game-license/), [Online Nevada](https://www.onlinenevada.org/articles/four-queens-and-fremont), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremont_Hotel_and_Casino)
- Railroad Pass 1931, Carson Nugget 1954, Riverside 1966, Colorado Belle 1987 (closed 2020) — see candidates file.

## Sceptical and alternative pass
Golden Nugget: Online Nevada shows McAfee's vice background but "never charged" in the 1931 matter; PBS says Kefauver's report had only four pages on Las Vegas. A Mob Museum piece calling the Nugget "gangster-built" could not be opened. For the four clean-labelled venues no allegation turned up, but no regulatory page was opened either.

## Corrections
- El Cortez: label 'none' and "legitimate local investors from opening" are wrong; Siegel's group owned it 1945-46 (alleged).
- Colorado Belle: closed 2020, not open; built by Circus Circus, not local investors.
- Carson Nugget: not family-owned throughout (sale chain 1956, 1958, 2015).
- Fremont: 'documented' stands on the 1979 commission action; the "convictions" claim is unchecked.
